03 · START
Style
The clean avatar is put together from the team's characters; every other style is rendered from your photo. Pick one to start from; your edits will stay on their own layer.
CLEAN AVATAR
—
Clean avatar is put together from the team's 40 characters, never traced from the photo. Custom (the default) takes the hair (with its head, ears and shoulders) closest to the photo's hair and keeps that character's face, swapping in glasses, a beard or moustache, closed eyes or a grin when the photo shows them; change any part in 08 parts. Prebuilt shows one character exactly as drawn: "auto" is the closest to your photo (dashed); step through them or pick one. Changing a part of a prebuilt character makes it custom, starting from that character. The characters exist at 25, 50, 72, 80, 96 and 128 stitches square, so the grid snaps to those sizes while this style is selected.
PARAMETERS
Image-driven avatar keeps the face paper with a one-cell contour, makes dark hair one solid mass (light or curly hair becomes strands) and draws every feature as a small shape: pupils, lids or dashes for narrowed eyes, continuous glasses rims, a moustache bar and goatee when the photo has them. Line weight thickens or thins the model's lines (ML lines). Cleanup removes lone cells that disagree with almost all of their neighbours (in the avatar: specks in curly hair); drawn features are protected. ML lines downloads a 15 MB line model once, then runs on this device.
04 · START
Regions
Each region gets its own cut, so any face stays readable.
SKIN
HAIR
—
CLOTHES
0 keeps a region open (paper), 1 fills it with ink, 0.50 is each style's own balance. Skin is normalised first, so the slider means the same on every skin tone. Even light removes the left–right imbalance of a side light (one half of the face in shadow) and keeps symmetric shading such as beards; 0 shows the photo's lighting as it is. Hair: solid is one mass with its larger highlights, tone uses four pattern levels, texture draws the outline and strand lines (best for curls); auto keeps each style's own hair. Hair lightness is measured from the photo, so light hair gets less ink than black hair at the same density.

Background
Pattern fills the background with evenly spaced dots; photo keeps the real background, drawn in the same style. Density sets how much of it is ink. The outline is an ink line against white and a one-cell paper gap against everything else.

Erase returns a cell to "auto" (whatever the style says). Bake flattens style + edits into plain pixels and freezes them; tick "live" to follow the style again (undo brings the bake back). Select: drag out a rectangle, drag inside it (or use the arrow keys) to move it; the cells it leaves take the colour around the selection, or paper. Flip turns the selection over where it is; mirror copy puts a flipped copy on the other side of the face centre (one eye onto the other). Moved cells become edits; one move is one undo step. Keys: B pencil · E erase · G fill · L line · S select · ⌘A select all · arrows nudge (⇧ 5 cells) · H / V flip · Esc deselect · X swap colour · M mirror · ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z undo / redo · + / − / 0 zoom · ⌘ + scroll zoom.
08 · EDIT
Parts
The face's parts, one row each. Tap a part in the view, or its name here, to see every option on your portrait.
Image-driven avatar and the other photo styles draw each part from the face points. Every option is shown as your portrait would look with it, in the selected style; pick one to use it, "auto" to let the style decide, "off" to leave the feature out. Auto, image-driven avatar: pupils behind glasses, lid + pupil without, dashes for narrowed eyes; one-row brows (two where the real brow is that thick), lifted above glasses rims; a D-shaped grin for an open smile, a smile or a line otherwise, and the moustache standing in for a closed mouth; a small nose except behind glasses; glasses shaped and sized like the frames when the photo shows them all round. The options were drawn as about twenty candidates each and the awkward ones taken out.
Clean avatar: every option is a part of one of the team's characters, named after it, placed on your hair's face at its eyes, nose and mouth and fitted to what is around it (brows kept off the hair and the frames, a gap around the mouth in a beard). Hair + head swaps the hair together with its head: face outline, ears, neck and shoulders. Only parts that fit this face are offered, and parts that look alike at this size are shown once. Auto keeps the hair's own parts and swaps in what the photo shows: glasses, a beard, goatee or moustache, closed eyes, a grin. A beard drawn into a character's jaw stays with that hair.

10 · MAKE
Export
One pixel per stitch.
FILE
SESSION
Saved in this browser. Only the last project is kept.
Every file holds exactly what the editor shows (edits and a lifted selection included): one pixel per stitch, no margin, grid or labels, every pixel opaque, in exactly your two colours — pure black and white unless you change them. PNG is a 1-bit indexed file. BMP bit depth is your choice: 1, 4 or 8-bit store the colours as a palette, 24-bit stores plain RGB; depths too small for the palette are disabled. SVG merges each row's runs into paths, with cells drawn at the cell aspect. The project (photo, settings, edits) stays in this browser until you forget it or load another photo.
